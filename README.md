## Topic
Our topic is non-compoundable criminal proceedings from matrimonial disputes decided by the Bombay High Court between 1 September 2021 and 31 August 2026. This topic covers the quashing of Section 498A IPC (and corresponding Section 85 BNS) charges under Section 482 CrPC (and Section 528 BNSS) following matrimonial settlements or false implication. It is a recurring and vital issue as thousands of families face prolonged criminal trials from matrimonial discord, making the High Court's exercise of inherent quashing powers a critical safeguard
## How we found the judgments
We searched the Bombay High Court eCourts portal and Indian Kanoon using search terms such as "quashing of FIR 498A", "matrimonial dispute settlement section 482 CrPC", and "cruelty quashing Bombay High Court". Together these queries returned roughly 50 to 60 potential judgments. We screened out 35 to 45 cases that were either non-reasoned interim orders, bail applications, or matters outside our date window, leaving us with 15 reasoned judgments. We kept a judgment if it was delivered within our date window, provided reasoned legal analysis, and directly adjudicated a quashing petition under Section 482 CrPC / Section 528 BNSS arising from matrimonial cruelty (s. 498A IPC / s. 85 BNS). We excluded routine remand orders, cases without full text, and judgments lacking substantive doctrinal discussion. The source link for each judgment in the dataset points directly to the official eCourts PDF copy.
## What the data shows
Judgments per year. Five of our 15 judgments were decided in 2022, four in 2023, two in 2024, one in 2025, and three in 2026 (up to August). We were surprised that we found no judgments from 2021 in our filtered search results.
Outcome. The quashing application or petitioner got the main relief in 12 judgments and partial relief in 2 judgments. Only 1 application was dismissed, which surprised us because it highlights how heavily the High Court favors quashing matrimonial criminal proceedings once parties reach an amicable settlement or when allegations are found to be general and omnibus.
Type of case. Fourteen judgments were registered as Criminal Applications (APL) under the inherent jurisdiction of the High Court, and one was filed as a Criminal Writ Petition. Most of these APL matters involve the quashing of Section 498A IPC charge-sheets and FIRs under Section 482 CrPC, which did not surprise us. What did surprise us was the extensive use of inherent jurisdiction to quash proceedings even after conviction during pending criminal appeals, as well as the recent transition to the new Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) and Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) frameworks in 2026.
## Limits
Our dataset of 15 judgments represents a curated sample retrieved through targeted keyword searches rather than the entirety of matrimonial quashing rulings across all benches of the Bombay High Court. A sample size of 15 is relatively small, meaning that adding or removing a few rulings would noticeably shift annual percentages. Furthermore, the dataset records qualitative legal outcomes and holdings rather than quantitative financial settlement amounts.
## AI-use disclosure
We utilized two AI tools to assist in workflow preparation. Claude (Anthropic) assisted in summarizing lengthy judgments, verifying citation formats, and drafting initial descriptive summaries. Gemini (Google) was utilized to synthesize the data analysis and generate the interactive HTML webpage code for the GitHub repository. We did not rely blindly on AI outputs. Every single judgment was independently reviewed from the official eCourts PDF copy, and all data entries, legal issues, holdings, and citations were cross-verified by team members against the primary source texts before publishing.
