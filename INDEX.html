Doctrinal Evolution & Key Thematic Trends

Synthesis of major jurisprudential shifts in quashing non-compoundable matrimonial proceedings under Section 482 CrPC.

1. Primacy of Amicable Settlement

Following foundational rulings like B.S. Joshi (2003) and Ramawatar (2021), the Bombay High Court consistently exercises inherent powers under Section 482 CrPC to quash non-compoundable Section 498A IPC proceedings once parties execute mutual consent divorces and financial settlements, holding that continuing prosecution serves no purpose when disputes are private and personal.

2. Protection Against Omnibus Implication

Citing Supreme Court precedents such as Kahkashan Kausar (2022), the Court actively scrutinizes charge-sheets and FIRs to protect extended family members (in-laws, sisters-in-law) from vague, omnibus allegations. Proceedings are routinely severed and quashed for relatives lacking specific attribution, while maintaining trial scrutiny for the primary accused.

3. Post-Conviction Quashing Powers

Demonstrating doctrinal flexibility, rulings such as Shaikh Shaukat (2023) established that Section 482 CrPC can even be invoked post-conviction while criminal appeals are pending before Sessions or Appellate courts, provided a genuine settlement (such as Khula and alimony) has been voluntarily reached.

4. Transition to BNSS / BNS Framework

With the enactment of new criminal law statutes, recent rulings like Akash Bhimrao Ingle (2026) seamlessly apply Section 528 BNSS (inherent powers corresponding to old Section 482 CrPC) and Section 85 BNS (corresponding to Section 498A IPC), ensuring continuity in judicial protection against arbitrary matrimonial prosecutions.
